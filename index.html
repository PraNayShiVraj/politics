01

Announcement

Setting the schedule & initiating ethical standards

The election cycle officially starts with the official **Announcement** made by the election commission or governing body. During this announcement, the specific dates for nominations, campaigning, polling day, and final counting are formally set.

Simultaneously, a set of guidelines known as the **Model Code of Conduct** is activated. This code is crucial because it ensures that governing parties cannot use public resources or official status to gain an unfair advantage, creating a level playing field for all political candidates.

02

Nominations

Candidates officially filing papers & screening

To run for office, candidates must submit their official nomination documents. This is a strict process supervised by the **Returning Officer**, the official responsible for overseeing the election in a specific **constituency** (electoral district).

During **Nominations**, candidates must pay a security deposit and submit a legal **affidavit**. This affidavit discloses key details such as their personal assets, liabilities, educational background, and any pending criminal cases, promoting complete transparency for the public.

03

Campaigning

Rallies, public messaging & spending regulations

Once nominations are approved, candidates start **Campaigning** through public rallies, advertising, and canvassing. Strict election laws govern this stage, including hard limits on campaign spending to ensure that wealth alone doesn't dictate election outcomes.

This stage concludes with the **campaign silence period**—typically starting 48 hours before voting begins. During this quiet time, all active public campaigning and political broadcasting must stop to give voters peaceful, undisturbed time to make their final decision.

04

Voting Day

Polling stations, cast casting & integrity measures

On **Voting Day**, eligible citizens visit their designated **polling station** to cast their vote. Voting is protected by a strict **secret ballot** system, ensuring that whom you choose remains completely private and free from intimidation.

At the polling booth, voters verify their identity, and officials apply a mark of **indelible ink** to their finger to prevent double voting. Votes are registered either on a physical paper **ballot** or electronically using an **EVM** (Electronic Voting Machine).

05

Counting

Securing boxes, tallies & oversight

After polling closes, voting machines and ballot boxes are sealed securely and transported to heavily guarded central locations. On **Counting** day, these containers are unsealed in the presence of official election staff and independent **observers**.

The counting progresses in structured, round-by-round tallies. Candidates and representatives of political parties are allowed to watch the counting process in person to confirm that every vote is handled fairly and accurately.

06

Results

Declaring winners & initiating transitions

Once counting finishes, the **Returning Officer** officially certifies the **Results** and declares the winning candidate. If candidates suspect irregularities, they can file official **election petitions** in court to audit the result.

Following result certification, the winning candidate or coalition of parties begins the process of government formation, preparing for the transition of power and taking their legislative oath of office.

Election Glossary

Here are definitions of key election terms you will encounter as you learn about the democratic process.

Model Code of Conduct
A set of guidelines issued by the election commission that political parties and candidates must follow during an election, especially regarding campaigning, speeches, and behavior. It takes effect as soon as the election dates are announced.
Returning Officer
The government-appointed official responsible for overseeing and conducting the entire election process within a specific constituency. They approve nominations and certify the final winners.
Constituency
A defined geographical area represented by an elected official. Voters residing within this boundary cast ballots to choose a single representative for their district.
EVM (Electronic Voting Machine)
An electronic device used to record and count votes securely. It is designed to be tamper-proof and speeds up the counting process compared to traditional paper ballots.
Secret Ballot
A voting method in which a voter's choices are completely confidential. It prevents coercion, intimidation, and bribery by ensuring that no one else knows who the citizen voted for.
Ballot Paper
A sheet of paper containing the names of the candidates and their political party symbols. Voters mark their preferred choice in private and drop it in a sealed box.
Indelible Ink
A semi-permanent purple ink applied to a voter's finger at the polling booth. It contains silver nitrate, which stains the skin and remains visible for weeks, ensuring individuals cannot vote multiple times.
Election Petition
A formal legal challenge filed in court questioning the validity of an election result. It is typically launched if a candidate suspects massive irregularities or voting fraud.

About ElectIQ

Strictly Non-Partisan Education

ElectIQ is a specialized conversational tool created to teach the public how democratic elections are conducted. Our guiding mission is to strengthen democracy by making election rules, timelines, and rights easy for everyone to understand.

Our Non-Partisan Pledge: ElectIQ never endorses, favors, or criticizes any political party, candidate, or ideology. We express zero personal opinions on policy outcomes. If asked a politically loaded or partisan question, we will guide you back to the facts of the electoral process itself.

Real-World Examples

ElectIQ provides global context. It can explain and compare different democratic systems, such as:

  • India: The largest democratic exercise in the world, managed by the independent Election Commission of India (ECI) using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs).
  • United States: A decentralized system where state laws govern voting, relying on the Electoral College for Presidential elections, primaries, and mail-in ballots.
  • United Kingdom: A parliamentary democracy governed by a First-Past-The-Post voting system where the monarch invites the winning party leader to form a government.
